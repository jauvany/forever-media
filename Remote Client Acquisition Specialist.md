## 3. Daily Schedule (Example — adjust to your working hours)

**Morning Block (Deep work: 2–3 hrs)**
- Review yesterday's replies first — always respond to warm leads before doing new outreach
- Build/refresh 20–30 new leads for the day (prospecting)
- Personalize and send 20–40 cold emails or DMs
- Log every touch in your CRM

**Midday Block (1–2 hrs)**
- Engage authentically on X/Instagram/YouTube: comment on target prospects' recent posts, react to their content — this warms them up before or after a cold touch
- Follow up on messages from 2–4 days ago (most replies come from follow-ups, not first touch)

**Afternoon Block (1–2 hrs)**
- Book/take discovery calls
- Send proposals or portfolio links to interested leads
- Update pipeline stages, note objections for pattern-spotting

**End of Day (30 min)**
- Log daily metrics: sends, opens, replies, calls booked, deals closed
- Plan tomorrow's target list

**Weekly (not daily):**
- Review which channels/scripts are converting best and double down
- A/B test one new subject line or opener per week
- Clean/refresh your lead list, remove dead contacts

## 4. Outreach Channel Playbook

**Cold Email**
- Keep it under 80 words. Lead with something specific about *their* content, not your agency.
- One clear CTA: "Worth a quick look at 2 sample clips I made from your last episode?"
- Follow up 3–4 times over 10–14 days before moving on.

**X (Twitter) DMs**
- Best for streamers/podcasters who are active posters. Engage with 2–3 of their tweets first, then DM.
- Reference something specific they said recently — generic DMs get ignored or reported.

**Instagram DMs**
- Works well for creators/coaches. Voice notes or short Loom-style video DMs outperform text.

**YouTube Comments → DM/Email**
- Leave a genuinely useful comment on a recent long-form video, then follow with an email referencing it.

**Warm outbound (referrals)**
- After every closed client, ask for one warm intro. This channel converts far higher than cold — treat it as a standing weekly task, not an afterthought.

## 6. Things NOT to Do

- **Don't mass-blast generic templates.** Zero personalization = spam filters + ignored DMs. Every message needs at least one specific, true detail about the prospect.
- **Don't skip the warmup on cold email domains.** Sending cold at volume from an unwarmed domain tanks deliverability fast — use a dedicated sending domain, not your main business email.
- **Don't pitch before providing value.** Sending a sample clip or genuine insight before asking for anything converts far better than "interested in our services?"
- **Don't over-promise numbers.** Never guarantee follower counts, viral views, or specific growth — promise process, quality, and consistency instead.
- **Don't ignore platform rate limits.** Instagram/X will flag or restrict accounts that send too many DMs too fast — stay well under platform thresholds and space sends out.
- **Don't neglect follow-up.** Most replies come from touch 3–5, not touch 1. A single-touch strategy wastes most of your pipeline.
- **Don't buy sketchy scraped lists.** Bad data tanks deliverability and wastes your day — build/verify lists instead of buying bulk unverified ones.
- **Don't go silent after a "not now."** Tag them for a 60–90 day re-engagement instead of deleting the lead.
- **Don't neglect compliance.** Include an unsubscribe/opt-out on cold emails and follow basic anti-spam practices (CAN-SPAM/GDPR depending on your prospects' region).
- **Don't treat outreach as separate from proof.** Always have your most recent, best clip example ready to send — stale portfolios kill conversion.

# Full Step-by-Step Execution Plan: Remote Lead Generation & Client Acquisition for a Video Clipping Agency

This plan provides a structured, actionable framework for generating leads and acquiring clients for a video clipping agency. The approach combines strategic positioning, targeted outreach, and systematic follow-up to build a consistent pipeline.

## Phase 1: Foundation & Positioning (Before You Send a Single Message)

### 1.1 Define Your Offer & Ideal Client Profile

**What You're Selling:**
A video clipping agency transforms long-form content (podcasts, webinars, YouTube videos, interviews) into a high-volume stream of short-form clips for TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and X. The value proposition is turning existing content into an "always-on presence" that generates organic-feeling reach at scale.

**Narrow Your ICP (Ideal Client Profile):**
Instead of targeting "everyone with video content," niche down. High-value targets include:
- **Podcasters** with 20-60 minute episodes
- **YouTube creators** with 10+ minute videos
- **Webinar hosts** and **course creators**
- **Brands** with existing long-form marketing content
- **Record labels/artists** promoting music through clips

The key is identifying clients who already have a backlog of content sitting underutilized—this is your "pain point" to address.

### 1.2 Build Your Proof Page (The Trust Asset)

A clipping agency sells results, not services. Your proof page should showcase:
- **Viral clip case studies**: Show original content → clipped version → performance metrics
- **Before-and-after transformation**: Long podcast → 30-second viral clip
- **Performance metrics**: Views, engagement rates, follower growth
- **Client testimonials**: Specific, results-oriented quotes

This page answers the visitor's core question: *"Can this agency actually get results?"* Without proof, even the best pitch falls flat.

### 1.3 Create a High-Impact Demo Reel

Build a tight 30-second reel showing transformation—the "before vs after edit" that demonstrates your clipping capability. Clients respond to visual proof of craft.

## Phase 3: Outreach Execution (The Client Acquisition Phase)

### 3.1 The Multi-Channel Approach

Successful outreach uses a mix of channels. Don't rely on one.

**Channel 1: LinkedIn**
- Send connection requests with a brief, value-focused note
- Post content about clipping results to demonstrate expertise
- Use InMail for warm leads

**Channel 2: Email**
- Use tools like **Instantly** or **SmartLead** for sequences
- Keep emails short, personal, and results-focused
- Include a link to your proof page or demo reel

**Channel 3: Video Outreach**
- Tools like **BombBomb** or **Dubb** let you send personalized video messages embedded in emails
- Video email has been shown to boost response rates significantly compared to text-only outreach
- Record a short (under 2 minutes) clip showing how you'd improve their content

**Channel 4: Direct Message with Value**
- **Don't say**: "Need an editor?" 
- **Do say**: "I noticed your podcast episodes could become 10-15 daily clips. Here's how I'd approach it and what results you could expect."

### 3.2 Outreach Message Template

**Subject**: Quick idea for [Their Content/Platform]

Hi [Name],

I noticed your [podcast/YouTube video/webinar] on [Topic] and saw an opportunity.

With [Number] minutes of content, you could be generating [10-20] daily clips for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts—driving consistent organic reach without more production work.

I help creators turn existing content into a daily distribution engine. Here's a quick example of what that looks like: [Link to proof page or sample]

Would you be open to a 15-min chat about this?

Best,
[Your Name]

### 3.3 Follow-Up Cadence

**Day 1**: Initial outreach
**Day 3**: Follow-up (value-add—share a tip or observation)
**Day 7**: Final follow-up (offer a free clip audit)
**Day 14**: "Closing the loop" (move to nurture sequence)

> "The key to having clips find their way into consumers' data feeds is to identify a 'hook' for the first one to two seconds of video. 'Think of it like fishing.'"

**Apply this to outreach**: Your hook is the first line of your message—make it count.

## Phase 6: What NOT To Do (Critical Pitfalls)

### ❌ Don't: Send Generic, Copy-Paste Messages

Personalization matters. Generic outreach is ignored. Use tools like Clay to personalize at scale, but every message should feel thoughtful, not transactional.

### ❌ Don't: Pitch Without Proof

Clients want evidence, not promises. Without case studies and results, even the best pitch falls flat. Your proof page should be your strongest asset.

### ❌ Don't: Chase Views Over Long-Term Growth

Sell "consistent, compounding visibility," not just "virality." Clients increasingly value campaigns that build lasting audience growth, not short-term spikes. Structure your offer accordingly.

### ❌ Don't: Forget to Niche Down

"Be 'the go-to editor' for something (music videos, podcasts, fitness reels—pick one)." Generalist outreach is less effective than targeted, niche-focused messaging.

### ❌ Don't: Ignore Follow-Ups

Most sales happen after multiple touches. Build a systematic follow-up cadence and stick to it.

### ❌ Don't: Overlook CRM Hygiene

Outdated contacts and messy data waste time. Keep your CRM clean and updated daily.

### ❌ Don't: Say "Need an Editor?"

Frame your outreach around value and outcomes, not just service listing. Show them *what you'd fix* or *what results you'd deliver*.

## 4. The "Do Not Do" List (Fatal Mistakes to Avoid)

* **❌ DON'T blast unverified emails:** Sending thousands of cold emails to unverified addresses will quickly blacklist your agency's domain, destroying the entire outbound operation.
* **❌ DON'T pitch features; pitch outcomes:** Do not say "We provide 4K editing, color grading, and text animations." The client doesn't care. Say: *"We turn your existing 1-hour podcast into 20 assets that pull in an extra 100k+ organic views per month."*
* **❌ DON'T use generic templates without context:** Saying "Hey, love your content" to a channel with 2 million views screams automated spam. Name a specific guest, a specific quote, or a specific problem you noticed on their feed.
* **❌ DON'T give up after one touchpoint:** 70% of bookings come from follow-ups 3, 4, or 5. Space your follow-ups 3–4 days apart. Keep them brief: *"Hey [Name], just checking if you had a chance to post that free clip I sent over?"*
* **❌ DON'T overlook the client's current branding:** Pitching aggressive, flashing "Alex Hormozi-style" captions to a high-end, elegant corporate B2B brand shows you haven't researched them. Tailor the style of the sample to match their aesthetic.

1. **Domain and Tool Infrastructure Setup:** Days 1–3.
Purchase 2–3 lookalike domains. Set up Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 accounts. Link them to Smartlead or Instantly and turn on email warming.


2. **Target Segmentation Design:** Days 4–5.
Define your exact sub-niches (e.g., Mid-tier real estate podcasters in the US). Build your first lead list of 500 contacts and run it through NeverBounce.


3. **Asset Generation and Testing:** Days 6–7.
Draft 2 distinct script angles (one performance-based, one gift-first). Gather 3-5 elite case studies or portfolios from your agency's editors to use as proof links.


4. **Campaign Launch and Gradual Scaling:** Day 15+ (After warmup).
Begin sending 10 emails/day per inbox, stepping up by 5 daily until you reach 35–40 emails/day per account. Initiate your manual social media DM tracking alongside it.

## The Omnichannel Sequence Blueprint

```
[Day 1: Instagram DM] ──────> [Day 3: LinkedIn Connect] ──────> [Day 6: Instagram Follow-up] ──────> [Day 9: LinkedIn Multi-Media Value]
  (The Pattern-Interrupt)      (The Context-Link + Soft Pitch)     (The Zero-Friction Ask)        (The "In-House Team" Alternative)

```

---

### Step 1: Instagram DM (Day 1)

**Goal:** Hook attention using a pattern-interrupt that proves you actually consumed their content. Keep it conversational, not salesy.

> **Message:**
> Hey [First Name], just caught your episode with [Guest Name]. Your breakdown on [Specific Topic/Insight] was incredibly sharp.
> Spotted a 45-second window around the halfway mark that would crush as an IG Reel if it had a punchier hook. Ever thought about chopping that specific segment up?

---

### Step 2: LinkedIn Connection Request (Day 3)

**Goal:** Move the conversation to a professional platform while referencing your previous touchpoint to instantly bypass their "cold pitch" filters.
*Note: Keep this under the 300-character LinkedIn limit.*

> **Connection Note:**
> Hey [First Name], dropped you a note over on IG about your episode with [Guest Name]—that segment on [Topic] was top-tier. Wanted to connect here to keep an eye on your upcoming guest lineup! - [Your Name]

---

### Step 3: Instagram DM Follow-Up (Day 6)

**Goal:** Deliver undeniable, hyper-personalized visual value. You aren't asking for a meeting yet; you are asking for permission to show your work.

> **Message:**
> Hey [First Name], I know your inbox is probably a circus, so instead of just talking about that clip, my team and I went ahead and edited it for you.
> We optimized the pacing, formatted it for mobile, and added engaging captions. Mind if I drop the Google Drive link over to you here? No strings attached, you can just have it.

---

### Step 4: LinkedIn Message (Day 9)

**Goal:** Change the angle. If they haven't replied, pivot to a low-friction "asset package" pitch that addresses their potential constraint (time or in-house resources).

> **Message:**
> Hey [First Name] – dropping back in here. I know you're heavily focused on pushing out the main long-form episodes right now.
> If you already have a team handling your content internally, no worries at all. I actually put together a quick, 2-page **"Short-Form Hook Framework"** document that we use to get [Metric, e.g., 50k+ views] on podcast clips.
> It's a completely free guide your current editor can use to boost your viewer retention this week. Would it be useful if I dropped the PDF link over to you?

## Execution Execution Reminders

* **Handle the DMs Safely:** Don't paste the exact same message to 50 creators on Instagram in one hour, or Meta will temporarily shadowban your account. Vary the first sentences and space them out.
* **The "Link Permission" Rule:** On Instagram, never send a raw link (like Google Drive) in your *very first* message if they don't follow you back. It often pushes your message straight into the hidden "Requests" folder or hides the text entirely. Always ask for permission to send the link first (as done in Step 3).
* **Log the Hand-Off:** If a podcaster replies on Instagram saying *"Yeah, send the link over!"*, immediately move them to your CRM as a **Warm Lead** and make sure your team drops the asset within 2-3 hours while the lead is hot.
